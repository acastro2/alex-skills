# Comms (email and Teams channel posts)

Two shapes: **email** and **Teams channel post** (top-level only). Channel replies are not comms. They follow [chat](chat.md), with no separate register.

Both shapes are short. Email always gets its frame ("Hi," and "Thank you,"); channel posts mostly go bare.

## Email

94% of his emails are replies. Median body 17 words. 59% to 61% are one sentence or less. Write replies first, new threads second.

### Frame: always "Hi," and "Thank you,"

Alex's rule (2026-10-05): every email opens with "Hi," or "Hi [Name]," and closes with "Thank you,", even a one-line reply or a forward. His sent mail shows the frame on 52% to 56% of emails and none on 28% (mostly forwards and alert replies), but he wants it on all of them, and his wish wins over the old habit.

> Hi,
>
> Yes, tonight from 10pm to midnight.
>
> Thank you,

- Greeting ends in a comma: 121 of 128. Bare "Hi," is 56% of greetings, so the name is optional. "Hi [Name]!" only to welcome someone new (4 of 128, all welcomes or first contact).
- Never "Dear", "Good morning", "Hope you are well", "Best", "Regards", "Cheers", "Sincerely" (all 0).
- Do not tie the name to group size. Do not rewrite the subject on a reply.
- The frame does not make the body longer. The body is still the short point.

### Content rules

1. **Point first.** The first sentence is the answer, decision, news, status, FYI, or ask (80%). Reason comes after it. Start with Yes, No, I, This, Can, or Thank. No "Cool", "Well", "So", "Now" openers.
2. **One ask, last, as a direct question.** "Can you ...?", "Can you please ...?", "Do you have ...?", "Any concerns?" 59 of 67 ask mails have exactly one "?". For a proposal, end on a plain consent check ("Is that acceptable?").
3. **End on a fact or that one question.** 53% to 55% of mails end on a plain statement, 25% to 27% on a question. No recap.
4. **"!" only on thanks, good news, "done", "it worked", and welcomes.** About 1 mail in 6 has one. Never on an ask, a correction, or bad news. Why: 70% to 73% of his "!" sentences are thanks, reaction, welcome, or result; ask-first mails carry one 5% to 7% of the time.
5. **No tags.** No "right?", "make sense?", "no?" (0, 1, 0 in 219 mails). "correct?" only to confirm a fact he just stated (2 mails).
6. **"I think" only as a fact hedge**, next to a date, cause, or number (8 mails, 6 of them hedges). State opinions flat.
7. **"Just" frames, one per mail.** "Just to confirm,", "Just letting you know ...", "I just wanted to ...", "just FYI" for a side note. "just" is in 13% of mails.
8. **Apology:** "Sorry I missed this," in the first line, one clause of reason at most, then the fix or the question in the same mail. No "my bad", no promise. 9 of 9 apologies carry a fix or ask.
9. **Contractions:** write "I will" in full. Keep "I'm", "don't", "doesn't". Do not use "I'll", "we'll", "you're", "we're", "I've". Evidence: email I will 16 to 2 over I'll; chat we are 315 to 3, you are 329 to 5; channel 45 full forms, 0 contracted.
10. **Sentences about 12 words** on average. Do not stretch to 24.
11. **No CAPS emphasis. Parentheses** only for a short factual clarifier (4% of mails).
12. **Shape:** one to three plain sentences, one paragraph. A plain dash list only for items to collect (times, names, files). No numbered list, no bold header per item. Past 60 words only for a handover, a multi-part answer, or an explanation to an outsider.
13. **"Let me know if you have any questions."** only on long or first-contact mail (offers are 4% to 6% under 60 words, 35% at 60 or more). A specific offer with a named action is fine. Never offer a call or meeting; put the answer or next step in writing.
14. **Stock phrases that are real:** "Can you please ...?", "Thank you very much", "I would like to ...".

### New thread exception

New threads are 9 to 10 of 219 mails. They run longer (median 34 words against 18 for replies). The reason or background may come first (4 of 9). The subject is a topic label, not an action or a deadline (0 of 10 give one). The one ask still goes last. Greeting and "Thank you," apply.

### Forwarding safety

Policy rule, not from the corpus. SKILL.md links here for forwardable exec email.

For exec-facing email that can be forwarded: no network identifiers, no named team-level risk, no defensive-security specifics. Say the topic exists and will be covered in person, with the real reason for deferring.

## Teams channel post

Top-level posts only. Median 12 words. 62% are 15 words or fewer, 27% are 16 to 40, 10% are over 40 (n=106 of his own posts). Why short: the long, listed, bold posts in the corpus were AI-drafted, not his typing.

1. **News first.** The first words are the news: "Enabled X", "Pushed X", "Created X", "I added X". A reason only if it changes what the reader does (5 of 32 news posts).
2. **Link alone on its own line, and last.** 24 of 27 posts put it on its own line, 21 of 27 last. A lead-in line may end in a colon.
3. **Greeting follows length.** None at 15 words or fewer (11% greet). Above about 16 words, "Hey all," or "Hey [team]," is the form (79% of greetings), with the news on the same line. It is optional: about 1 post in 3 at 16 to 40 words. No "!" after it. No "Hi [Name]!".
4. **No closer, no signature.** Comma closer 0 of 106, signature 1. "Thank you" only on a request to another team (4 of 106).
5. **At most one "!", on the last line**, for news, a win, or thanks (9 of 12 posts that have one). Not in the body, not on an ask.
6. **One ask, near the end, with a relative time:** "today", "this week", "before the meeting", "when you can". No calendar deadline unless Alex gives one (0 of 106). Put the "?" in the last or second-to-last line.
7. **Capital first letter.** Lowercase starts (10%) all sit on posts of 15 words or fewer. "I" is always capital.
8. **No end period** on one-liners (0 of 23 at 5 words or fewer, 1 of 43 at 6 to 15). Above 16 words a final period is allowed (6 of 29 at 16 to 40), but most such posts end on a link line, "?", "!" or no mark.
9. **No lists, bullets, bold, or emoji.** The only structure is a lead-in line, a blank line, and the link.
10. **Length:** 15 words or fewer by default. 16 to 40 only for news with a link. Over 40 only if Alex asks. No sign-off, no recap.
11. A name or team tag, then a short ask on the same line, is fine (about 1 post in 5 starts with a name and no greeting).
12. Do not use the long "Hi all! ... Thank you," announcement shape in channel posts. His own typing never uses it. If Alex wants a formal cross-org announcement, write it as email.
13. No swearing, no "lol", no "ok so", no tag question in a post (swearing 0 of 448 channel replies; tags 1.1%).

Anything longer than a few lines that lands in a Teams channel pastes as an HTML fragment. The compose box ignores pasted markdown. Follow [Teams delivery](../teams-delivery.md).

## Red flags

- An email with no "Hi," or no "Thank you,".
- "Hi [Name]!" to someone who is not new.
- A numbered list, a bold header, or "etc!" at the end of a list.
- "!" on an ask or a correction.
- "One quick tip" (one onboarding mail, not his habit).
- Any offer of a call or meeting ("hop on a call", "we can talk it on a call", "book a call", "let's sync"). Alex works async: put the answer or next step in writing. The checker blocks these.
- Any "right?", "make sense?", "no?" tag. Two "I think".
- Generic closing offers: "let me know if you need anything else", "want me to help further?", "happy to help". An offer that names one real action is fine. "Let me know" alone is real.
- "Moreover", "Hence". Do not use. They are rare in his own mail (3% to 6%) and not part of his voice.
- "Cool", "Well", "So", "Now" as an opener.
- A reason before the answer on a reply.
- Channel: a greeting on a post of 15 words or fewer, a signature, a closer, bullets or bold, a calendar deadline, more than one "!".
