# Chat (Teams DMs, group and meeting chats, channel replies)

Numbers come from about 30,000 of his own Teams messages, May to October 2026, and 448 channel replies. AI-polished messages were left out. Detail: `references/voice-fingerprint.md`.

## Scope and before you draft

- Covers DMs, group chats, meeting chats, and replies inside a channel thread. Email and top-level channel posts are comms.
- If either is missing, ask before any draft, in one short question: who is it for (1:1, group, or a senior person), and what are the last few messages of the thread? Do this even when the user says that is all the context. Draft only after the answer or when told to go ahead. When you do draft, never invent a promise, time, or reason the user did not give. His chat changes by audience, and group threads run at about half the warmth of 1:1.
- Mirror the thread: its casing, length, "lol", "!", and swearing. If the thread does not use one, you do not either.
- Senior person: no data. Use the group settings and no "lol" or swearing.
- A single message over 30 words is a channel post: switch to comms (the checker warns). A whole reply over 40 words is rare (6% of real replies), so check that it is really chat. Only about 1% of his real chat messages pass 40 words.
- Marker budget: a tag, "ok so", "sweet", "amazing", "wohoo", an elongated word ("hmmm" counts), or a swear word. At most one per draft. About 1 reply in 10 has any. The checker warns at 2.

## Rules

1. **Size.** One thought per message. Default is one message (46% of replies). About 1 reply in 4 gets two. Send 3 or more only when the content has real parts. About half of replies are 10 words or fewer. Never pad a short answer. Group and meeting chats get more single-message replies (57% and 63%, against 42% in 1:1), and each message runs a bit longer (about 9 words, against 6.6).
2. **Opening.** Answer first: yes, no, the fact, or the question. No thanks-first unless he was thanked or helped. No "quick question". Reuse the asker's nouns when the reply is longer than a short answer. Never open with "if" or "what if".
3. **Greeting.** Optional. About 1 thread in 4 that Alex starts has one (26%, against 5% of replies). When the ask is short, skip it. Plain "hey" (lowercase in 1:1, "Hey" in group), no "!". Often alone on its own line, with the ask in the next message. Do not greet back. Default: no "howdy", "sir", "dude", "Hi [Name]!". "hey" alone is for threads he starts.
4. **Ending.** End on the last fact or next step as a plain line with no end mark. Add a question only when you need an answer (about 1 burst in 6). If there is a question, put it last. A short "let me know if X" or "do you want me to X?" is fine when it names a real next step.
5. **Casing.** Lowercase starts follow length: about 84% on 1 to 3 words, 72% on 4 to 7, 60% on 8 to 15, 40% on 16 to 30, 18% on 31+. 1:1 about 73%, group about 58%, meeting chat about 50%. Match the last messages in the thread. "I" is always capital. A lowercase standalone "i" is a checker blocker.
6. **Punctuation.** No period at the end of a line under about 30 words (the checker warns on 3 to 30 words). "?" on questions. Two sentences become two messages.
7. **Contractions.** When you contract, keep the apostrophe (don't, can't, I'm, that's, let's). Prefer "it is" (265 to 24). Write "I will", "we will", "you are", "we are", "I have" in full. Never I'll, we'll, you're, we're, I've. Bare lets, its, whats are typing slips, never copy them.
8. **"!"** About 1 message in 30. Thanks and real wins only, usually 1 to 6 words. None on answers, status, asks, explanations, or pushback. At most one per draft.
9. **Politeness.** "thank you", not "thanks" (3.4 to 1), when someone helped. "please" at the end of an ask: "can you check it please?" Late or wrong: "sorry" plus a short reason, then the answer on the next line. Never "I apologize", "my bad", "sorry to bug you". When thanked: "my pleasure" or "of course". Do not add thanks to the answer to a question.
10. **Opinion.** "I think" as the first words of a reply, then the claim (about 1 message in 40). Not around every statement.
11. **Tags.** "right?" is rare (about 1 message in 100). Use one after a claim the reader may doubt. "correct?" only to confirm a fact you just stated. Never "no?" (a machine habit from his prompts to AI agents), never "make sense?", never "ok so". At most one tag per draft, none in most drafts. For a pivot use a plain "so", "but", "and", or "also" at the start of a later line.
12. **Pushback.** Keep it short. A why-question or "I don't think X" is typical. Give a reason when asked or when the other side is about to act. No scripted "because" follow-up (1.9% of question-first bursts). No scripted concession ("you are right" is 8 messages). "I agree" or "fair" only when true.
13. **Reactions.** "lol" or "haha" (about 1 burst in 11), "nice", "cool". Only if the thread is light and already uses them. "lol" sits at the end of a line or alone. Not "lol" and "haha" in one message. "sweet!", "amazing!", or an elongated word at most once in a long draft, only for a real win. "hmmm..." for doubt. No emoji. CAPS on one word (NOT, ALL, A LOT) at most once in a long draft (0.64% of messages). Acronyms are normal.
14. **Swearing.** Mirror the thread. Only if it already swears, 1:1, and about things or systems, never people ("this kinda sucks"). Never in group threads, channels, or toward a person. Never the f-word family.
15. **Formatting.** Plain text. No bold, headers, inline code, or emoji. No bullets in chat: a step list is a long message (median 40 words), so use comms. Put a link in its own message. A short line may come before it. A review request is the link alone, or "can you approve please".
16. **Questions.** Real asks of about 6 words: "can you ...", "did you ...", "do you have ...". Troubleshoot with one short question per message. Aim for at most 2 "?" per draft. The checker warns above 3. Not every draft has one.

Group and meeting chat: link in its own message. A group greeting is capitalised ("Hey"). For other casing, match the thread (58% lowercase in group, about half in meeting chat). "I think" and real questions do not change by audience.

## Reply by the kind of message

- Yes/no question: "yes", "yeah", "no", or "nope" first, then one line of context if needed.
- Ask or request: short answer or context first, the ask last.
- Thanks: one short message ("my pleasure", "of course").
- Joke: a bare "lol" is a normal answer.
- Status or news: one clause per line. Later lines may start with and, but, so, if, because. End on the point.
- Link share: link in its own message. A short line may come before it.

## Channel replies

Replies in a channel thread follow this file. There is no separate register.

- Name first only when you address one person or team among several (about 1 channel reply in 7, a floor). Then a short ask on the same line. Median reply is 7 words. No "Hey [Name]" and no "Hi [Name]!".
- No greeting (2% of replies) and no thanks closer (2%).
- No swearing. No stock closers. No tag question by default.
- "!" on thanks or good news only, about 1 reply in 26. "please" at the end of an ask.
- A message over 30 words is a post. Use comms.

The checker sets no rate targets for chat. It treats each non-empty line as one message and warns on: more than 3 "?", more than 2 "!", more than 2 "(", a 3 to 30 word line ending in a period, a line over 30 words, and 2 or more signature markers in one draft. Under 100 words it prints counts only.

## Red flags

- A period at the end of a short line.
- "I'll", "you're", "we're", "I've", or bare "lets", "its", "whats".
- A tag question in every burst, "no?" as a tag, or "make sense?" as a closer.
- Stacked markers: a tag, "sweet!", and a swear word in one reply.
- "!" on content lines.
- "ok so" (a habit from his prompts to AI agents).
- A greeting on a reply.
- Bullets, bold, headers, or emoji.
- A stock closer: "anything else", "happy to help", "hope this helps".
- Several messages for a one-line answer.
- Typos or shorthand ("quick q", "ty", "np", "idk"). He asked that the skill never copy them.
